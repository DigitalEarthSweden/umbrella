Frequently Asked Questions

Digital Earth Sweden Map Operation

Where is the Digital Earth Sweden Map User Manual?

There is no separate user manual. The map is designed to be self-explanatory, and the main features are described in this Help: see Getting started, Map Navigation and Display, Data Catalogue and Data Workbench.

The Digital Earth Sweden Map is built on the open-source TerriaJS platform. The general TerriaJS user documentation also applies.

What browsers work with Digital Earth Sweden Map?

Use a current version of a modern browser such as Chrome, Firefox, Edge or Safari. The 3D views require WebGL, which all of these support. If WebGL is unavailable or disabled, the map falls back to the 2D view.

What types are spatial data are displayed by Digital Earth Sweden Map?

The Digital Earth Sweden Map displays two kinds of spatial data on top of its base maps:

Raster layers – images served as map tiles, for example satellite imagery (Sentinel-2), elevation models and thematic maps from national agencies. Many of these are time series: use the time slider to step between dates.

Vector features – points, lines and polygons such as water bodies, boundaries and monitoring stations.

Click on the map to see more information about the feature or pixel at that location.

Why do "3D Terrain" and "3D Smooth" Perspective Views look the same on my computer?

Some older computers do not have graphics cards which fully support WebGL and some older browsers do not make use of this support even if it is present. Digital Earth Sweden Map requires this support in order to support 3D Terrain Perspective View.

Try upgrading your browser to the latest version. If that still does not work, you may need to consider working on a computer that has more recent graphics support.

Why does the terrain in my perspective view have two different hues?

Imagery layers are often mosaics assembled from several satellite passes, acquired on different dates and under different lighting and atmospheric conditions. Adjacent scenes are not colour matched to each other, so neighbouring areas can have noticeably different hues. This is a property of the source data rather than of the map.

When you are using Perspective View the part of the terrain nearest to the "camera position" is naturally shown at a larger scale than the more distant parts. Depending on your zoom level, this can result in the foreground being displayed with a different set of photography from the distant parts of your Perspective View.

Does the Digital Earth Sweden Map work on mobile devices?

The map works on phones and tablets with a simplified interface, but some features (for example the 3D views and large data sets) are demanding. Performance depends on the device's graphics capability and memory, so results vary between devices. For the full experience use a desktop or laptop computer.

Digital Earth Sweden Map system and data

Where does the spatial data come from?

The data comes from Digital Earth Sweden's own processing of Copernicus Sentinel data and from Swedish agencies and organisations such as Lantmäteriet, SMHI, Havs- och vattenmyndigheten, Naturvårdsverket and MSB. Each data set in the catalogue names its provider. Click About this data on a data set in the Data Workbench to see its description and source.

Where do the base maps come from?

The base maps displayed by the Digital Earth Sweden Map come from Natural Earth, NASA's Earth Observatory and Lantmäteriet.

Who developed Digital Earth Sweden Map?

The Digital Earth Sweden Map was developed by RISE. See the About page from more information.

How reliable is the spatial data on the Digital Earth Sweden Map?

The reliability will vary between data sets. To understand the reliability of a particular data set, click on the title of the data set in question in the Data Catalogue for more information. The metadata which is shown in the Data Catalogue may offer sufficient information or you may need to contact the listed data provider.
